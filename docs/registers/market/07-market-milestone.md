## Shape

Opens with a "remember when" contrast. The register frames today
against what used to be surprising:

    "It was big news when the value of Apple (AAPL) surpassed
     $1 trillion. And heads turned when Nvidia (NVDA) reached
     the trillion-dollar club in record time. But now a dozen
     S&P 500 companies have done it."

Then the finding, with its source:

    "Twelve S&P 500 companies, including Nvidia, Apple and now
     Advanced Micro Devices (AMD), sport market values of $1
     trillion or more, says an Investor's Business Daily
     analysis of data from S&P Global Market Intelligence and
     MarketSurge."

Then the implication, with a number that matters:

    "It's just a reminder of how top-heavy the S&P 500 has
     become. Those 12 companies are worth a total of $30.7
     trillion. That's nearly 44% of the value of all 500 stocks
     in the S&P 500."

Then a company spotlight on the newest entrant:

    "Advanced Micro Devices, an innovative chip-design firm,
     closed at $615.52 a share on Monday. That's a breathtaking
     187% jump this year."

Then the rest of the group, briefly profiled.

Then the table. The table is the deliverable — the reason a
reader keeps the page:

    | Company Name        | Ticker | Market Value | YTD % |
    | Nvidia              | NVDA   | $5.5T        | 21.8% |
    | Apple               | AAPL   | $4.9T        | 24.7% |
    | ...

Then a closing line with the takeaway, often framed as a
warning dressed as a reminder:

    "Just know the S&P 500 isn't as diversified as you might
     think it is."

## Person

Third person throughout, but bylined. The register has a named
author ("Follow Matt Krantz on X").

No first person. No second person.

## Length

500-800 words, plus the table.

## Ending

The table, followed by a one-line takeaway. The takeaway is a
statement, not a question, and it lands on a caution:

    "Just know the S&P 500 isn't as diversified as you might
     think it is."

## Forbidden

    - A number-first opening. The register always opens with
      the "remember when" contrast.
    - Any first-person or second-person address
    - A summary close
    - A question as the closing
    - A table without a preceding narrative that sets up its
      significance
    - Causal language without attribution

## Sample markers

From the sample article:

    - "It was big news when the value of Apple (AAPL)
       surpassed $1 trillion."
    - "says an Investor's Business Daily analysis of data from
       S&P Global Market Intelligence and MarketSurge"
    - "Those 12 companies are worth a total of $30.7 trillion.
       That's nearly 44% of the value of all 500 stocks in the
       S&P 500."
    - A table titled "Most Valuable S&P 500 Stocks"
    - Closing line: "Just know the S&P 500 isn't as
       diversified as you might think it is."
    - Bylined ("Follow Matt Krantz on X"), with a "YOU MAY ALSO
       LIKE" section at the bottom.

## Scope

Whole market, driven by a threshold or first-of-its-kind event.
The table is the deliverable. Not for routine days (use
wire-close), not for single names (use ticker-alert).
