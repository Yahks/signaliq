# research-screen

    Pool:     market
    Source:   "The Russell 2000 (^RUT) is home to many small-cap
              stocks, offering investors the chance to uncover
              hidden gems before the broader market catches on."
              — a research-product screen presenting verdicts on
              several names
    Markets:  US

## Shape

Opens with the segment and its framing. The register names the
hunting ground and the risk before it names any stock:

    "The Russell 2000 (^RUT) is home to many small-cap stocks,
     offering investors the chance to uncover hidden gems
     before the broader market catches on. However, these
     companies often come with higher volatility and risk..."

Then it states the verdict as a count:

    "...here are three Russell 2000 stocks to avoid and better
     alternatives to consider."

Each company gets its own block:

    - A header line: Company Name (TICKER)
    - A market cap line
    - A one-sentence description of the business
    - A verdict subhead, framed as a question:
        "Why Do We Steer Clear of MEC?"
        "Why Do We Think GBX Will Underperform?"
        "Why Are We Bearish on LGIH?"
    - Three bullet reasons, each a specific negative fact with
      a number:
        "sales have declined by 3.7% annually over the last
         two years"
        "gross margin of 12.2%"
        "7× net-debt-to-EBITDA ratio"
    - A valuation line at the end:
        "Mayville Engineering is trading at $17.18 per share,
         or 25.3x forward P/E."
    - A soft CTA:
        "Read our free research report to see why you should
         think twice about including MEC in your portfolio."

## Person

Institutional "we" — the research firm — and second person "you"
— the reader/investor.

    "Why Do We Steer Clear of MEC?"
    "Read our free research report to see why you should..."

The register is authored by the firm, addressed to the reader.

No first-person singular. No "I".

## Length

500-700 words.

## Ending

The last company's block ends, then a soft CTA. The register
does not close on a summary. It closes on the funnel.

## Forbidden

    - A narrative opening
    - Any first-person singular
    - A hard rating ("Buy", "Sell"). The register gives
      verdicts ("avoid", "steer clear"), not ratings.
    - An opinion that isn't attributed to the firm's analysis
    - Causal language without a number behind it
    - More than 5 companies in one piece
    - A summary close

## Direction

The register is direction-neutral. The sample is bearish
("three stocks to avoid"), but the same register runs a
positive version ("three stocks to buy"). The verdict
direction is a parameter:

    bearish (default in the sample) — "avoid", "steer clear"
    bullish — "worth a look", "our top pick"

Everything else — the segment framing, the per-company
structure, the bullet reasons, the funnel — stays the same.

       "Read our free research report..."

## Scope

A few names (3-5), verdict-driven, research-product shaped.
Not for whole-market (use wire-close), not for single names
(use ticker-alert or stock-analyzer).
