# retail-recap

    Pool:     market
    Source:   "The American consumer has been carrying this
              economy for months. Friday morning brought two
              reports suggesting the load is getting heavy." —
              a retail-facing mid-session market recap
    Markets:  US

## Shape

Opens with a narrative frame, not a number. A character or a
tension is set in the first two sentences, and the numbers
arrive after the reader is already inside a story.

    "The American consumer has been carrying this economy for
     months. Friday morning brought two reports suggesting the
     load is getting heavy. Stocks responded with a slow,
     unglamorous slide."

Then the index numbers, with a timestamp:

    "The Nasdaq Composite is down 0.44% as of 12:00 p.m. ET..."

Every data point is compared against expectations:

    "Retail sales dropped 0.6% in July... Economists had
     forecast a 0.1% gain."

There is a "technicality" section. The register explains why the
obvious reading of a number is wrong, and gives the real reading:

    "Some of that is a technicality. Amazon moved Prime Day to
     June this year, which pulled sales out of July..."

The biggest single drag is named with its dollar value:

    "Broadcom fell 5.6% and gave up roughly $105 billion in
     market value, the single largest drag on both the S&P 500
     and the Nasdaq."

## Person

Third person throughout. One first-person scan of the tape,
stated as an observation, not as opinion:

    "I don't see a single move of 2.5% or more on the Dow today."

No second person. The register speaks about the market to a
reader, not with a reader.

## Length

400-500 words.

## Ending

Closes on the detailed state of one index or one sector, not a
summary. The final paragraph is a scan of the tape, not a
conclusion.

## Forbidden

    - A number-first opening ("Stocks closed down 0.3%...")
    - Any second-person address
    - More than one first-person sentence
    - A summary close
    - A question as the closing
    - Causal language that isn't paired with a named source

## Sample markers

From the sample article:

    - "The American consumer has been carrying this economy
       for months."
    - "as of 12:00 p.m. ET" (timestamped mid-session)
    - "Economists had forecast a 0.1% gain."
    - "Some of that is a technicality."
    - "Broadcom fell 5.6% and gave up roughly $105 billion in
       market value, the single largest drag on both the S&P
       500 and the Nasdaq."
    - "I don't see a single move of 2.5% or more on the Dow
       today."
    - Ads and image placeholders embedded mid-article (the
       register expects interruption structure).

## Scope

Whole market, mid-session. Named-stock movers carry the piece.
Not for post-bell (use wire-close), not for single names
(use ticker-alert).
