# technical-strategist

    Pool:     market
    Source:   "It is hard to believe that the S&P 500 is only 2-3%
              below its all-time high" — an independent technical
              strategist's daily note
    Markets:  US

## Shape

Opens with a stance, not a number. The writer states a belief
about the market, then defends it with levels and structure.

    "It is hard to believe that the S&P 500 is only 2-3% below
     its all-time high, given the carnage across various parts
     of the market."

The register reasons in orders. It names the reasoning explicitly:

    "If you think about the market through a second-order lens..."
    "The logical third-order question then becomes..."

It anchors on specific levels:

    "For now, the S&P 500 is holding near the 6,800 level,
     supported by gamma positioning."
    "If 6,800 breaks, the next area of support would likely be
     between 6,700 and 6,720."

It references charts the reader is looking at:

    "SPX Chart"
    "VIX-VIX3M-Daily Chart"
    "NVDA-Daily Chart"

## Person

Second person sparingly, and only to invite the reader into the
reasoning:

    "If you think about the market through a second-order lens..."

First person: absent. The writer's voice is in the assertion, not
in an "I".

## Length

300-500 words.

## Ending

Closes on an open question, not a summary:

    "Now that software stocks are rolling over, the question is
     whether NVIDIA follows the same path."

The reader is left holding the question.

## Forbidden

    - A neutral report opening ("Stocks closed lower today...")
    - A summary close ("In conclusion...")
    - Defining technical terms (gamma positioning, backwardation,
      dispersion index, implied correlation, head-and-shoulders,
      neckline). The register assumes the reader knows them.
    - Attributing the view to "analysts" or "the market". The
      view is the writer's.
    - Causal language without a level or a mechanism behind it

## Sample markers

From the sample article:

    - "It is hard to believe that..."
    - "The logical third-order question then becomes..."
    - "At this point, the only thing that appears to be holding
       the market together is NVIDIA."
    - "A break below $170 would likely not only signal further
       downside for NVIDIA, but could also trigger a broader
       breakdown in the major indexes."
    - Chart references inline: "SPX Chart", "NVDA-Daily Chart"
    - Open question as the final sentence.

## Scope

Whole market or single large-cap with a market-wide implication.
Chart-driven. The register argues; it does not report.
