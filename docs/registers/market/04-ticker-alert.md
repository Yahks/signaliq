# ticker-alert

    Pool:     market
    Source:   "On September 29, 2026, Societe Generale reported
              that the Utilities sector has underperformed the
              S&P 500 by nearly 30%..." — a single-symbol
              automated alert on a research platform
    Markets:  US

## Shape

Opens with a trigger. Date, source, event. The piece exists
because something happened — a bank report, an upgrade, a
price move, an earnings release.

    "On September 29, 2026, Societe Generale reported that the
     Utilities sector has underperformed the S&P 500 by nearly
     30%, even as the 10-year Treasury yield climbed above 5%."

The first paragraph is keyword-dense. It contains the symbol,
the metric names, the source, and the reason a search would
land on this page.

Every section heading is a question the reader might ask:

    "What's Behind the News?"
    "Is XLU's Dividend Safe and Attractive?"
    "What Does XLU's GF Score™ Tell Us?"
    "What Are Gurus and Insiders Doing with XLU?"
    "What This Means for Investors"

Branded metrics appear with trademark symbols. The register
has its own framework and names it (GF Score™, GF Value™,
or whatever the platform's equivalent is).

A section on institutional activity:

    "7 premium gurus hold positions in XLU, with 4 having
     added shares and 2 trimming in recent quarters."

A table for the branded score breakdown:

    | Metric            | Rating      |
    | GF Score™         | 76          |
    | Financial Strength| 3.2 / 10    |
    | Profitability     | 7 / 10      |
    | Growth            | 7 / 10      |
    | Valuation         | 9 / 10      |
    | Momentum          | 4 / 10      |

An FAQ section formatted as Q&A for search snippet pickup:

    "What is XLU's GF Score™?"
    "Is XLU's dividend safe?"
    "What is XLU's P/E ratio compared to historical?"

A disclosure line at the end:

    "This stock alert was generated using automated technology
     and..."

## Person

Third person throughout. The register speaks *about* the stock
to a reader.

No first person. No second person. The reader is addressed
implicitly ("For income-focused investors..."), not as "you".

## Length

600-800 words, longer than the others because of the FAQ.

## Ending

A "What This Means for Investors" section. Soft advice, never a
hard rating:

    "Investors seeking dividend stability in a volatile interest
     rate environment should consider monitoring XLU's evolving
     fundamentals."

Followed by the FAQ and the disclosure.

## Forbidden

    - Any first-person or second-person address
    - A narrative opening
    - A question as the sole closing (the FAQ is formatted as
      Q&A, but the register always ends on the CTA, not a
      question to the reader)
    - A rating ("Buy", "Sell", "Hold") — the register gives
      signals, not ratings
    - An opinion attributed to the writer. All opinions must
      be attributed to gurus, analysts, or the platform's own
      metrics.

## Sample markers

From the sample article:

    - "On September 29, 2026, Societe Generale reported that..."
    - "XLU offers a 3.6% dividend yield with a conservative
       payout ratio of 60%, supported by a healthy 3-year
       dividend growth rate of 8.01%..."
    - Section headings as questions
    - "GuruFocus data shows 7 premium gurus currently hold XLU,
       with 4 adding and 2 trimming positions recently"
    - "This stock alert was generated using automated
       technology and..."
    - FAQ section at the bottom

## Scope

One symbol, reactive. Written in response to a trigger. Not
for whole-market pieces (use wire-close or market-milestone),
not for multi-name sector pieces (use sector-picks).
